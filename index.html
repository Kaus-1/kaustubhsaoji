I use computation to design proteins and peptides that bind therapeutic targets, and I want those designs to work at the bench, not only on screen.

I’m a final-year dual-degree student at BITS Pilani, K K Birla Goa Campus (M.Sc. (Hons.) Biological Sciences and B.E. Electrical and Electronics Engineering). I’m currently doing my undergraduate thesis in Dr. Madduri Srinivasarao’s lab at the Purdue Institute for Drug Discovery, where I’m building an in silico pipeline for the de novo design of peptide binders for immunotherapy. The pipeline uses generative and structure-prediction models such as BoltzGen and Boltz to generate candidates, predict peptide–target complexes, and rank designs for synthesis and experimental validation.

My route here has crossed several fields. I started in the wet lab, isolating high-purity RNA from horseshoe crab embryos. I co-authored a review on cold atmospheric plasma for skin cancer (Drug Discovery Today, 2025), ran quantum-chemistry and docking studies for drug repurposing, and trained spiking neural networks on EEG data. Protein design is where these threads meet: biology that matters, models that generate, and engineering that makes the loop between them fast.

Research interests

  • De novo protein & peptide design
  • Generative models for biomolecules
  • Binder design for immunotherapy
  • Structure prediction & design triage
  • Design–build–test loops
  • ML for drug discovery

In a PhD, I want to work on making generative protein design more reliable: understanding why designs fail when they reach the lab, building better filters and scoring signals for picking candidates, and closing the loop between computational design and experimental data. I’m especially drawn to binders and peptides for therapeutic targets, where design goals go beyond affinity to include specificity, cross-reactivity and developability.
